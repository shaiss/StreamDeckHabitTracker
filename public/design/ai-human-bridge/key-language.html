The OLED Key Language

Interactive artifact · companion to the AI ⇄ Human Bridge design study

The written study fixes a small grammar; this page renders it. Each key is a semantic tile that answers — in under a second, the same way every time — whose turn (the frame), what (the glyph + label), what a press does (the verb), and how urgent (the motion). Press the keys. Toggle Desaturate to confirm the states survive without color, and Reduced motion to see the brightness-only fallback.

Two accessibility checks from the study (docs 2.2 & 5.2).

State owns the frame; object identity owns the interior; agent identity owns the dot. Three zones, three jobs — that firewall is what lets an amber "your move" frame light up without touching the glyph that says what is waiting.

The state color system doc 2.2 — color + motion, redundantly encoded

Color is reserved almost entirely for turn-state. Each hue has exactly one job, and each state is carried twice — color and motion — so the meaning survives when the color can't. Hit Desaturate: the breathing, blinking, and shimmer still tell you whose turn it is.

Amber is the signature. A dark deck with one amber key breathing is the canonical "you're needed" picture. Blocked and danger share a red but not a channel: blocked is a key's state (frame), danger is an action's property (glyph).

The glyph grammar doc 2.5 — one verb, one glyph, everywhere

Glyphs name the verb — what pressing does — so the affordance is legible before the label is read. Filled means active/running; outline means available. A small, teachable set.

Interaction patterns doc 3 — composed from the tokens above, nothing new

These are the "components" of the language: named, learn-once shapes for the recurring moments of supervision. Press them — every committing press flashes white (the deck saying "got it") before the frame shows what your press did.

This is a rendering, not the product. It exists so the grammar in the study can be seen and felt instead of imagined. The study's open questions (doc 5) — how much a 72px key really carries, how loud is too loud, whether the states survive real colorblindness testing — are exactly what a prototype on this repo's real key faces is positioned to answer.
